# Animal-video trend recipe research

**Research date: 19 October 2026. Window: 19 October 2025–19 October 2026.**

The catalog contains **32 documented formats or revivals, plus two explicitly unverified requested variants**: sink cats and beach cats. Older origins qualify only where sources document a new wave inside the window. This is an evidence-based research shortlist, not a live platform leaderboard.

The requested “That’s private property” appears in the documented **State Property / Hit the Road Dude** family, with an October 2026 revival. “Migos Hotel Lobby” is the **Quavo & Takeoff Hotel Lobby AI** trend; the source performance is older, while the AI wave is documented in September 2026. Both are included.

**Read the data correctly:**

- Linked social posts were located in inspected explainers, embedded posts and source footnotes. TikTok/Instagram playback and current counters were not consistently accessible, so this report does not claim a frame-by-frame video audit. Link objects distinguish example posts from explainers and include dates and verification limits.
- Each entry’s `notes` begins with the **source-described format**, followed by evidence and limitations. `length_s`, `shots`, all cut times, camera instructions, captions, and the first-second hook are **proposed original animal adaptations**, not measured source timestamps. Exact original duration, cuts and BPM are marked **unverified**.
- `audio.title` and `audio.artist` identify the reference sound. `genre`, `bpm`, `mood`, `instruments`, and `hook_at_s` are **a proposed brief for your new music**. The hook time refers to the proposed edit. These numeric BPMs are not claims about the reference recordings.
- `licensed: true` means an identified released commercial work; it **does not mean licensed for your app**. `false` denotes a dialogue/creator-sound reference, not public-domain status. `null` means commercial/licensing classification is unverified. No source audio is cleared by this report.
- `peaked` names an evidenced wave and explicitly marks the exact maximum unverified. `alive: true` means a dated example within the preceding 30 days was located; `null` means current activity is unverified, not that the trend is dead. A new vendor article or a template still being available is insufficient by itself.
- Engagement figures are dated **source-reported snapshots**, not independently audited current totals. Views, likes, sound uses and template uses are kept distinct. Sound/template uses are not unique creators. Unverifiable vendor-wide aggregate estimates were excluded.
- **Feasibility: 5 is easiest**, 1 hardest. Scores assume editable multi-shot output, separate character layers, captions added in post, and original or licensed motion references. They are planning estimates, not model benchmarks. Human-to-animal motion retargeting can distort paws, tails and short legs.
- **Virality:** 5 = very large recent spread with several examples; 4 = substantial documented participation or a large older wave; 3 = narrower, older or weaker participation evidence; 2 = limited traction; 1 = unverified. These are editorial evidence scores, not predicted views. Source confidence and recency are stated per entry.

**Coverage limits:** Closer has one dated 2026 revival example and two explicitly historical examples. The Sway origin timeline conflicts within its source and is marked unverified. Face Dancing has a dated 2026 resurgence but some individual example dates are unknown. The sink and beach entries have no verified pair of original posts and are excluded from ranking. The June Three Kittens and August La Calabaza entries are related but distinguishable song/routine waves; beach backgrounds are not counted as another established trend.

Current dance waves such as Toc Toc, La Calabaza, Bulin 47 and Smalltown Boy are useful second-wave additions after multi-character motion works reliably.

## Production and rights boundary

Build reusable engines for a reaction loop, fixed duo performance, walking reveal, interview setup/payoff and hard-cut transformation. Put all captions, precise cuts, wipes and zooms in the editor. Keep generated clips focused on one action, and use separate character renders when choreography would otherwise cross bodies.

New footage and music do not by themselves answer every rights question. Individual steps and simple gestures differ from an expressive choreographic work; an entire routine may require separate assessment. This distinction is described in the [U.S. Copyright Office’s choreography guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ52.pdf); it is not a worldwide clearance ruling. Use original models, dialogue and music, and motion references you are entitled to use. The per-entry risks identify source characters, real-person likenesses, sensitive meanings and distinctive scenes to avoid.
