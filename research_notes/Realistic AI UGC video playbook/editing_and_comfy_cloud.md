## (A) What editing and finishing recipes make UGC ads perform and feel authentic on TikTok / Reels / Meta in 2026?

### Takeaway
The official platform guidance is short and consistent: 9:16, sound on, a native/DIY (not over-polished) look with a human on camera, the proposition in the first 3 s and the hook inside 6 s, text at 5–10 words per second inside the UI safe zone, a clear CTA, and 3–5 *very different* creatives per ad group. Everything more specific (ideal length, B-roll %, cut rate, animated word-by-word captions) is practitioner lore and vendor data: directionally consistent, but rarely verifiable, and sources disagree on length (9–15 s vs 15–30 s vs 21–34 s vs 30–60 s for Feed).

### Cited Findings

**Platform guidance (primary)**
- TikTok's performance-ad guidance: orient 9:16, shoot at least 720p, keep content inside the UI safe zone "as per ad specifications" (no pixel values given), "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds", "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds", use sound or music, use captions/text overlays for context — "We recommend displaying 5-10 words per second when using text" — and use transitions, stickers and graphics to retain view time. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Same page: feature creators, employees or customers; use a "DIY or not overly polished look" to match TikTok's UGC; lean into trends/memes; end with "a clear, strong call-to-action". The page gives no recommended length and no quantified lift stats. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Testing volume per TikTok: "between 3-5 different creatives per ad group and 3-5 diversified ad groups per campaign", with *big differences* between creatives during exploration; refresh when delivery declines (no fixed schedule); add new creatives to an existing ad group rather than starting a new one; keep a creative asset library. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Meta (as relayed by Adobe, Nov 2024 — dated): Reels creative that pairs 9:16 sound-on video with at least one extra element (human presence, text overlay, voice-over, or an early hook) saw 20% lower CPC, 56% higher CTR and 93% higher ThruPlay rate across placements. — [Adobe Blog, Reels best practices (2024)](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud)

**Length (sources conflict)**
- Several blogs attribute a "21–34 s sweet spot" for in-feed ads to TikTok's Creative Center, one pairing it with a "280% conversion-rate lift vs <10 s" claim; I could not find the Creative Center original, so treat as unverified. — [Selzee](https://selzee.com/blog/tiktok-ads-best-practices); [Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-video-length/)
- Contradicting windows: 9–15 s "is king" for direct-response, 21–34 s for awareness — [Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/how-long-should-a-tiktok-ad-be); 15–30 s conversion sweet spot — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-ad-creative-best-practices-2026); 9–15 s completion sweet spot — [MB Adv](https://www.mbadv.agency/tiktok-ads/creative-best-practices); TikTok's in-feed spec reportedly allows up to 60 s with 15–20 s recommended for engagement — [Triple Whale TikTok ad specs](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/tiktok-ad-spec.md)
- Motion/Runneth: Facebook suits "longer-form storytelling (30–60 seconds)" with problem→solution narrative; TikTok gets "quick cuts". — [Runneth (a Motion product), How to create effective UGC ads](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Placement split from an agency: Feed = raw testimonial/demo, 15–45 s, problem→solution; Reels = hook-first 9:16 under 30 s. — [AI Advantage Agency](https://aiadvantageagency.com/ugc-ad-creative-for-facebook/)

**Structure and hooks (first 1–3 s)**
- Common structure: Hook → Problem → Solution → CTA; one guide times it as a 2–3 s hook, pain point by ~5 s, product solving it by 10–12 s, then CTA. — [DesignRevision](https://designrevision.com/blog/ugc-ads-without-creators); [Biddy Co](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/ultimate-guide-ugc-ads)
- Motion/Runneth hook frameworks with sample openers: problem–solution ("I used to struggle with…"), transformation ("Here's what happened when I tried…"), social proof ("Everyone's been asking about…"); "3 seconds to stop the scroll"; test "3-5 different opening approaches per product"; track performance at creative *and* creator level. — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Write 3–5 hook variations per concept; hooks can be provocative/contrarian; the hook should land in the first second. — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads); [Biddy Co](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/ultimate-guide-ugc-ads)
- A Reels "decision" time of ~1.2 s before a viewer scrolls is cited by a benchmark vendor; not verifiable against Meta. — [Benly, Meta ads creative specs 2026](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/meta-ads-creative-specs-2026)
- Reddit-sourced roundup: AI UGC works when the hook is strong and the script names a specific, recognisable pain point; generic AI UGC ("this product is amazing, buy now") performs poorly. — [Tagshop](https://tagshop.ai/blog/what-are-ai-ugc-ads/) (vendor)

**Pacing, jump cuts, B-roll**
- Cut every few seconds using angle changes, location changes, different people or B-roll; close-ups, unboxing and in-use shots hold attention; show the product rather than a static talking head. — [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/raw-ugc-to-ads); [ORM Solutions](https://ormsolutions.uk/ugc-video-ads/editing/)
- Editors remove filler sounds/pauses and use jump cuts instead of dissolves to keep the native, energetic feel. — [ORM Solutions](https://ormsolutions.uk/ugc-video-ads/editing/); [Editing Machine](https://editingmachine.com/blog/ugc-video-editing-for-ads-scaling-authenticity)
- Motion/Runneth gives no B-roll ratio or cut-rate target (only "quick cuts" for TikTok). — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Common mistakes: slow openings, scripted delivery pretending to be casual, talking heads for products that should be demoed; the creator should look like the buyer. — [Biddy Co](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/ultimate-guide-ugc-ads); [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)

**Native vs polished**
- Motion/Runneth: the sweet spot "looks intentional but not overproduced" — "iPhone quality with good natural lighting rather than a professional studio setup"; authentic ≠ bad lighting or shaky camera; ring lights are fine. — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- "UGC-style" in 2026 means shot on a phone *or made to look like it*; native creative beats polished on Facebook cold traffic. — [AI Advantage Agency](https://aiadvantageagency.com/ugc-ad-creative-for-facebook/); [NovoAds](https://novoads.ai/en/blog/ugc-style-ads) (vendor)
- Vendor benchmark: UGC beats polished on hook rate (+31%) and CTR (+33%) — Meta figures 34% vs 26% hook rate, 1.88% vs 1.41% CTR; polished still wins for higher-ticket purchases. Unverified vendor data. — [Benly ad creative benchmarks 2026](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/ad-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- Counterpoint: some brands report UGC fatigue; the format needs fresh execution. — [Motion, how to write UGC ad scripts](https://motionapp.com/blog/how-to-write-ugc-ad-scripts)

**Captions, on-screen text and safe zones**
- TikTok: 5–10 words per second for on-screen text (official). — [TikTok Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Don't caption every word with equal weight; pull out the strongest lines and give them emphasis. — [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/raw-ugc-to-ads)
- Active-word highlighting helps muted viewers most; bold concrete numbers (e.g. a price) as keywords. — [Captions.ai help: highlight keywords](https://captions.ai/help/guides/engagement/highlight-keywords) (vendor)
- Caption-tool vendors rank word-by-word highlighted captions top for 2026 and claim animated captions ~double retention vs static — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/video-caption-styles-ranked); [ClipSpeed](https://clipspeed.ai/blog/ai-captions-increase-views.html); but BlitzCut itself says the evidence is "murkier" than vendors admit (best data point is a 2019 Verizon/Publicis survey on captions in general; OpusClip's data comes from a caption-software seller). — [BlitzCut, animated vs static captions](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/animated-captions-vs-static-captions)
- Only research-grade study found (2023, language learners, not ads): time-synced keyword highlights were preferred for learning but felt too distracting for everyday viewing. — [arXiv 2307.05870](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.05870)
- The widely repeated "Meta found captions lift view time 12%" and "85% of Facebook video watched muted" appear only in secondary sources. — [AI Advantage Agency](https://aiadvantageagency.com/ugc-ad-creative-for-facebook/) (unverified)
- Reels safe zone (commonly cited, not an official Meta 2026 spec): top ~14% (name, follow, audio label), bottom ~35% (caption, buttons, CTA bar), sides ~6% — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/); the 20–35% bottom range = ~20% Stories vs ~35% Reels, and taller Android screens can crop edges, so use ~10% side margins — [Adnova](https://adnova.ai/blogs/meta-ad-safe-zones-guide)
- TikTok safe-zone numbers conflict (20% bottom/10% right vs 120 px top/bottom on 1080×1920), and TikTok doesn't publish pixel margins; check its downloadable templates. — [AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/tiktok-ad-specs); [Aliakbar Fakhri](https://aliakbarfakhri.com/tiktok-ads-specs/)
- Burn captions in; keep them inside the safe zone and off faces. — [UGC ABC TikTok specs 2026](https://ugcabc.com/blog/tiktok-ad-specs-2026)

**Music and sound**
- TikTok: trending audio/sounds; Reels: trending audio at lower volume under the voice-over; Facebook: clear voice audio with no music competing. — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- TikTok officially: "use sound or music". — [TikTok Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)

**CTA patterns**
- Verbal CTA as friendly advice ("If you're dealing with this same problem, definitely check this out"; "Use my code [CREATOR10] to get the same discount I got"); visual CTA: typing the code on a phone, holding the product with branding visible, pointing to the link, screen-recording the product page. — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)

**Variant testing and benchmarks**
- Keep body, CTA and length fixed while testing hooks; 3–5 variants per variable before calling a winner; read hook retention to judge openings; one UGC video can become a dozen ad units by changing hook, sequence, length, caption and CTA. — [LaunchPoint](https://www.launchpointhq.com/blog/re-cut-ugc-into-paid-ad-variants)
- Motion's 2026 creative benchmark study: ~550k ads, 6,000+ advertisers, ~$1.3B spend on FB/IG, Sep 2025–Jan 2026 (another summary: 578,750 creatives / 6,015 accounts). — [AdBeacon](https://www.adbeacon.com/2026-creative-benchmarks-are-out/); [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- Hook rate (3-s views ÷ impressions) thresholds attributed to Motion: <25% needs work, 25–35% solid, >35% strong; hold-rate definitions differ between sources (ThruPlays÷3-s plays vs 15-s÷3-s), so benchmarks aren't comparable. — [Sepia Lab hook rate](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks); [Sepia Lab hold rate](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hold-rate-benchmarks)
- Billo's account average ≈24% Meta hook rate; high hook + weak hold (38%/28%) scales fast then crashes within ~14 days; only ~5% of creatives become winners. — [Adlibrary hook rate](https://adlibrary.com/posts/hook-rate); [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)

**AI disclosure (affects how "authentic" AI UGC can be)**
- Multiple agency blogs report TikTok made an AI-disclosure label mandatory for *realistic* AI-generated ad creative from 21 July 2026 (synthetic voice-overs, AI product shots, generated backgrounds, cloned spokespeople), with escalating penalties up to a permanent ban, and auto-labelling from C2PA metadata. I did not find TikTok's own policy text. — [Stellar Search](https://www.stellarsearch.co.uk/insight/tiktoks-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-are-live-what-brands-running-ai-creative-need-to-do-now); [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-policy); [Common Thread Co](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/tiktok-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-ecommerce-2026)
- Meta: the "AI info" label is applied automatically to content from Meta's tools or C2PA-tagged files; sources disagree on whether ordinary commercial AI UGC must be manually disclosed; strictest enforcement is on political/social-issue ads; photorealistic AI presenters are the main trigger on both platforms. — [Cinerads, Meta policy](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy); [Cinerads, disclosure compared](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements)

### Inferences
- **A defensible default recipe for the app** (synthesised from the above, not a single source's claim): 9:16 1080×1920; a 0–3 s hook that states the proposition (pain-point line, transformation, or social proof) with a visual pattern-interrupt (product in hand, close-up, or reaction) on frame 1; pain point by ~5 s; product demo/B-roll in the middle; result + friendly verbal CTA (code/link) in the last 3–5 s, with an end card inside the safe zone. Ship 15 s, ~25–30 s and (for Feed) ~45 s cuts of the same body rather than betting on one length, since length guidance conflicts.
- **Pacing**: a visual change (jump cut, punch-in, B-roll insert, angle switch) every ~2–4 s with dead air/filler removed is what "quick cuts / cut every few seconds" translates to; a 30–50% B-roll share over the A-roll voice is a reasonable starting range, but no source gives a measured ratio (see Gaps).
- **Captions**: burned-in, speech-synced, 1–3 words per screen (keeps within TikTok's 5–10 words/s ceiling), active word or keywords/numbers highlighted, placed in the central band (below the ~14% top, above the ~35% bottom), never over the face or the product. Make the style a variant axis rather than a fixed rule because the "animated beats static" evidence is vendor-sourced.
- **Native feel**: the AI pipeline should *degrade* to phone-plausible (natural light, slight handheld motion, room tone, imperfect framing) rather than push cinematic 4K polish; upscaling should stop at clean 1080p.
- **Variants**: the cheapest axis to vary is the hook (first 3 s only) on a fixed body; generate 3–5 hooks × 1 body, which also fits TikTok's 3–5 creatives per ad group.
- **Disclosure**: an AI UGC maker should plan for an "AI-generated" label on TikTok (realistic AI presenters and cloned voices are squarely in scope) and keep C2PA/metadata honest; "authentic" must come from script, delivery and editing, not from passing as a real customer.

### Gaps
- No primary TikTok Creative Center source for the 21–34 s / 280% claim, and no official 2026 Meta Reels creative guide with stats (the Meta stats found are from 2024 via Adobe).
- No measured A-roll/B-roll ratio or cuts-per-second benchmark from any credible source; only qualitative "quick cuts".
- No independent study of word-by-word vs static captions in *ads*; the Equativ April 2026 muted-viewing study exists but its findings weren't retrievable. The 12% and 85% caption stats are unverified.
- Direct practitioner threads (r/FacebookAds, r/PPC, r/TikTokAds) weren't retrievable in search; the Reddit view here is second-hand via a vendor roundup.
- TikTok's official July 2026 AI-ad disclosure policy text was not found; details come from agencies.

## (B) Which parts of a best-in-class AI UGC pipeline can run on Comfy Cloud / ComfyUI today, and at what cost?

### Takeaway
Every generative stage is covered on Comfy Cloud: persona stills, product-in-hand, reference-locked characters, one-shot talking clips with native dialogue, audio-driven talking photos, lip-sync repair, TTS/voice clone, speech-to-text with **word timestamps (ElevenLabs Scribe v2 node)**, upscaling, interpolation and prompt-based video edits. What's missing is *assembly*: no template burns captions, sequences A-roll/B-roll or mixes music, so the app must do the edit itself. Costs per 5 s range from ~11 cr (open models on GPU time) through ~23 cr (HeyGen Video 1.0 i2v 768p) and ~148 cr (Kling 2.6 with audio) to ~865 cr (Seedance 2.5 1080p); per-second talking/lip-sync runs ~15 cr/s (HeyGen Talking Photo), ~21 cr/s (VEED Lipsync 2.0) and ~40 cr/s (Sync 3).

## (B) Which open ComfyUI workflows/models are used for realistic talking avatars and lip-sync in 2026, and how good are they vs partner APIs?

### Takeaway
The open stack used in ComfyUI is LTX-2.3 Image+Audio→Video (by far the most-used open lip-sync template on Comfy Cloud), Wan 2.1 InfiniteTalk / MultiTalk, Wan 2.2 S2V, HuMo and LivePortrait for generating talking video, and LatentSync 1.6 (community node, Apache-2.0) for dubbing existing footage. No rigorous 2026 head-to-head against HeyGen/Sync/VEED exists; the available evidence (one test-based write-up plus Reddit anecdotes) says open models are usable but less reliable — lip accuracy slips, motion is static, teeth/mouth artefacts and slow 720p inference — while partner APIs are the safer default for client-facing realism.

### Cited Findings
- Comfy Cloud ships open, GPU-time-only talking templates: LTX-2.3 Image Audio to Video (usage 1,044), LTX-2 Audio to Video (49), LTX-2.3 ID-LoRA (appearance + voice adaptation, 121), Wan 2.2 S2V (104), InfiniteTalk full-body dubbing (72) and a music lip-sync variant, HuMo (18), LivePortrait (22). By comparison HeyGen Video 1.0 i2v shows usage 841, Sync 3 lip-sync 81, Seedance 2.5 r2v 2,700. — [catalogue](/home/shiny/Documents/GitHub/ComfyMade/data/templates/index.json); [estimator](/home/shiny/Documents/GitHub/ComfyMade/data/templates/credits-2.json)
- Instavar's 2026 comparison (Wei Jie Chee, 22 May 2026; internal benchmark of short social-video dubbing fixtures judged on crop, compression, timing, identity and human review): **LatentSync 1.6** is the preferred local baseline for existing-video lip replacement; **KeySync** is the closest challenger but issue reports cite identity drift, artificial teeth, colour distortion and FPS/sample-rate sync errors; **MuseTalk** is a speed fallback (30 FPS on a V100) with mouth jitter/blur; **InfiniteTalk** is a heavier talking-video model, "not a drop-in swap", changes head/body/expression too, with reports of degradation outside the face, slow inference and 720p memory pressure; **LTX LipDub (LTX-2.3 IC-LoRA)** is watchlist, beta limits on clip length, language and teeth sharpness, gated community licence; Wav2Lip/VideoReTalking are legacy baselines (mouth-box artefacts; Wav2Lip licence complaints). No VRAM numbers or numeric scores published; HeyGen/Sync/D-ID are not benchmarked. — [Instavar, Open-source lip sync models compared in 2026](https://instavar.com/research/ai-video/open-source-lip-sync-models)
- Another 2026 guide recommends LatentSync 1.6 for final dubbing quality on close-ups (Apache-2.0, commercial use OK) and MuseTalk for real-time/fast drafts. — [tomodahinata, AI lip-sync model selection guide 2026](https://tomodahinata.com/en/blog/ai-lip-sync-talking-head-model-selection-guide-2026)
- InfiniteTalk in ComfyUI is built on MultiTalk (Wan 2.1), runs single image + voice → lip-synced talking video, offers FP8 (high-VRAM, best quality) and GGUF (low-VRAM) variants, and `audio_scale` raises mouth-movement strength; community workflows are on Civitai. — [Next Diffusion tutorial](https://www.nextdiffusion.ai/tutorials/create-lip-sync-videos-from-images-with-infinitetalk-in-comfyui); [Civitai InfiniteTalk workflow](https://civitai.com/models/2487266/infinitetalk-audio-portrait-to-lip-synced-video-in-comfyui); [Comfy workflow page](https://comfy.org/workflows/templates-wan2_1_infinitetalk_music-1eab7aa23f6a/)

- Partner-side capability claims for comparison (vendor descriptions in the catalogue): HeyGen Talking Photo adds expressions, head movement and automatic gestures (Avatar IV); Sync 3 Talking Image moves only lips/lower face and leaves hands/products/background untouched; VEED Lipsync 2.0 keeps the rest of the shot as recorded. — [catalogue](/home/shiny/Documents/GitHub/ComfyMade/data/templates/index.json)

### Inferences
- **Recommended split for a UGC maker**: draft and B-roll on open models (LTX-2.3 ia2v, Wan 2.2) at GPU cost; final talking A-roll on a partner API (HeyGen Avatar IV / Talking Photo for expressive full-head motion; Sync 3 or VEED Lipsync when the product in hand must not drift); LatentSync 1.6 is the open option for re-voicing already-good footage but isn't a Comfy Cloud template (a local/custom-node route).
- LTX-2.3 ia2v's high usage among Comfy users suggests it's the de facto open default for audio-driven talking clips in 2026, while InfiniteTalk/HuMo/S2V are niche; usage isn't quality, though.
- Open models' known failure modes (teeth, mouth blur, static body, identity drift, slow 720p) are exactly the tells that break "authentic UGC", so an app should QA close-ups and prefer medium shots with B-roll cutaways to hide weak lip-sync.

### Gaps
- No 2026 side-by-side (same portrait + audio) comparing InfiniteTalk, LTX-2.3 ia2v, Wan S2V, HuMo, LatentSync with HeyGen, Sync 3 and VEED; Reddit evidence is from January 2026, before LTX-2.5 (Aug 2026) and before the VEED/HeyGen Video 1.0 releases.
- MultiTalk, FantasyTalking, OmniAvatar, StableAvatar and newer Wan-based minute-long models (e.g. TalkVerse) weren't covered by any quality comparison found.
- No ComfyUI Discord or Civitai quality discussions retrieved beyond workflow listings.
