# Realistic AI UGC video ads in 2026: pipelines and the image-first (still) stage

Research date: 2026-10-23. Most sources are 2026 blog posts by tool vendors, agencies or prompt sellers. Very few are independent, and none is a proper blind study. Vendor pages are marked **[vendor]**. Where a claim comes only from a search-result summary (the page itself was not opened), it is marked **(snippet)**.

---

## 1. Image-first or video-first, and what the full pipelines look like

### Takeaway
Every 2026 pipeline I found that aims for realism works image-first. The persona is fixed as a still. Each shot then gets a still built from that persona (often matched to a frame from a real creator's video), and the still is animated with image-to-video or motion control. Text-to-video is used only for B-roll with no character in it. Two variants lead: (a) still → **Kling motion control**, driven by a *real* reference clip (Skaleit agency), and (b) still → **Veo 3.x / Grok Imagine** image-to-video, which produces its own speech for the talking shots (Retiplex).

### Cited Findings
**Pipeline A: motion copied from real footage (agency, self-reported results)**
- Skaleit Agency (Antonio Ventre, published 20 May 2026) runs five stages: 1) **Idea**: copy a proven concept from Facebook Ad Library, TikTok or Instagram organic, rather than inventing one; 2) **Script**: a 10–20 s hook-led format or a 30–45 s voiceover narrative; 3) **Clipping**: pull real reference clips from IG, TikTok or Pinterest that match the storyboard and split them into single shots; 4a) create **one AI avatar in Nano Banana Pro from a Pinterest reference photo**; 4b) **screenshot the first frame of each reference clip**, upload it with the avatar to Nano Banana Pro, and prompt it to recreate that frame's lighting, setting and composition with the avatar in place of the original person; 4c) in **Kling AI motion control**, use the original reference video as the motion source and the new first frame as the static input; 5) assemble in CapCut or Premiere and save every clip to a reusable B-roll bank — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- Skaleit's numbers (self-reported, unverified): 13 ads used 59 credits ($1.72), about **$0.13 per ad**, against $150–400 and 7–14 days for a human UGC video, and "up to 5x ROAS" on Meta. They give no verbatim prompts or settings, and they do not cover lip-sync — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- Skaleit lists the giveaways of other AI UGC tools as uncanny movement, robotic voices, **plastic-looking skin** and broken character consistency, and calls **voice "the single biggest tell"**. Their advice is a high-quality voice tool or a real voice actor — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- A similar stack: Nano Banana Pro or Flux Pro for character stills → Kling Motion Transfer → ElevenLabs voice → CapCut **(snippet)** — [adlibrary guide](https://adlibrary.com/guides/how-to-create-ai-ugc-ads-that-convert)

**Pipeline B: a still, then image-to-video with native speech [vendor-ish blog]**
- Retiplex (2026, exact date not given), 10 steps: 1) Claude reads 2 product photos and writes a plain-English "visual profile" (category, container, colours, finish, material, label text) that can be pasted into other tools; 2) Claude writes a script of about 80 words / 30 s (hook → agitate → reveal → proof → CTA, "Casual, like a friend talking to the camera"); 3) Claude writes a storyboard of 4 scenes (shot type, VO line, duration); 4) **Nano Banana** makes the creator portrait at 9:16, saved as the reference; 5) **Veo (via Flow) or Grok Imagine** image-to-video makes the talking clips; 6) **ElevenLabs** clones the voice *from a generated talking clip* and makes the full VO; 7) product B-roll: a Gemini still, then **Kling** image-to-video; 8) Audacity cuts silences and splices at word boundaries; 9) CapCut assembles; 10) CapCut auto-captions and exports **1080×1920 H.264** — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)
- Retiplex's case for image-first: video tools "work best when you hand them a starting frame", product shots improve with one, and the still fixes the face and the product before any motion is added. Talking clips use a model that generates its own speech, for better lip-sync — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)

**Other variants**
- Oakgen **[vendor]** (6 July 2026): "Lock the character in still images first, then move into video", and "Image-to-video gives the model a stronger starting point than text-to-video … usually more stable for ad characters" — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)
- Planning the shots as a **3×3 grid storyboard image in Nano Banana 2**, then animating with Kling 3.0 **(snippet)** — [AI Fire](https://www.aifire.co/p/nano-banana-2-kling-3-0-cinematic-ai-ad-workflow-2026)
- Automated version (n8n template): a product photo plus a person photo → a merged UGC still → prompts for the start and end frames → video model **(snippet, paid template)** — [Gumroad template](https://thesohailjafri.gumroad.com/l/ixdag)

**What the video models offer the image-first stage**
- Veo 3.1 can animate a start image, interpolate between a first and a last frame, or take **up to 3 reference images** to keep a person's or product's appearance **(snippet)** — [WaveSpeed comparison](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/wan-2-7-vs-seedance-2-vs-sora-2-vs-veo-3-1-fast-image-to-video-comparison/), [Novoads](https://novoads.ai/en/blog/seedance-2-vs-veo-3)
- On one UGC platform, Veo 3.1 always starts from a product or actor image and tends to win on the polish of a single continuous shot. Seedance 2.0 makes 5/10/15 s clips at 9:16 in 720p or 1080p, and its strength is taking many inputs. Sources disagree on whether Seedance takes a first frame, and they call Veo and Seedance roughly tied on realism **(snippet)** — [ugcvids.ai](https://ugcvids.ai/compare/veo-3-1-vs-seedance-2-0) **[vendor]**
- **Sora 2**: praised for UGC texture (imperfect framing, motion blur, believable rooms) and suggested for non-talking beats such as unboxings. It **blocks uploads that contain photorealistic people**, so it cannot animate a persona still. A 2026 DEV post says "Sora 2 is gone". Per the brief, the Sora 2 API shut down in September 2026, so treat any Sora-based pipeline as dead **(snippet)** — [Morphed](https://morphed.app/blog/ai-ugc-video-generator), [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sora2hubteam/best-sora-2-alternatives-for-image-to-video-in-2026-51bb), [Blue Lightning (Nov 2025, older)](https://bluelightningtv.com/2025/11/02/turn-a-single-product-photo-into-a-ugc-or-cinematic-ad-with-ai-artlist-sora-2-veo-3-1/)

### Inferences
- The most "real-looking" pipeline borrows from real footage twice: the **composition and lighting** of each still copy a real creator's frame (Skaleit 4b), and the **motion** comes from the real clip (Kling motion control). That removes the two biggest AI giveaways, staged framing and floaty motion. Whoever builds on this should know it relies on other creators' footage as reference, which has rights and platform-policy implications.
- Pattern by shot type: **A-roll (talking)** = persona still → a video model with native speech (Veo/Grok), *or* a still → motion control on a real talking clip, plus separate VO and lip-sync. **B-roll (product, hands)** = a product-accurate still → Kling image-to-video. Pure text-to-video only for faceless B-roll.
- Cloning the voice from the model's own generated speech (Retiplex step 6) keeps the voice of a talking shot and the VO the same, which matters given that "voice is the biggest tell".

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of image-first against text-to-video realism (both run on the same brief and blind-rated). The case for image-first is consistency and control, as practitioners report it.
- No verbatim Kling motion-control settings, and no lip-sync tool comparison in the sources I opened (voice and lip-sync are probably another researcher's scope).
- I found no first-hand Reddit threads (r/aivideo, r/PPC, r/FacebookAds). Searches returned blogs only.

## 2. Which still-image models give the most believable phone photos

### Takeaway
**Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image)** is the model most often named for photoreal skin and the "phone snap" look in 2026, and it wins the only head-to-head UGC-selfie round I found. **GPT Image 2** comes out "too clean and evenly lit" for UGC but is clearly better at text, so a two-model setup (NBP for the photo, GPT Image 2 for any typography or legible text) is common advice. Seedream 4.5/5.0 reads as more commercial or mockup-like, and FLUX 2 as polished and cinematic. On the open-source side, **Z-Image Turbo** is favoured for photorealism and LoRA training. None of the evidence is blind.

### Cited Findings
- **AI Video Bootcamp test** (22 Apr 2026, updated 30 Apr): the same 10 prompts went to `gpt-image-2` (OpenAI Playground, high quality, Thinking off) and `gemini-3-pro-image` (Google Flow), with no re-rolls or retouching. **Round 9, UGC selfie (9:16): Nano Banana Pro won**, with "a more natural phone-camera look". GPT Image 2.0 "looked too clean and evenly lit, and the ramen too stylized". **Round 7, hyperreal portrait: NBP won**: its skin texture, subsurface scattering and hair-light detail "read as a real camera frame", while GPT 2.0's portrait looked like "a strong digital painting". GPT 2.0 won most text-heavy rounds (manga, menu, poster). NBP **refused a prominent-person prompt**. The article recommends NBP for the base image and GPT 2.0 for typography — [AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-2026/)
- **AI Pro Photography lab** (tests run 5 Oct 2026, both models run in Higgsfield at 3:2 and about 2K): Nano Banana Pro against **Seedream 5.0 Pro**, 12 untouched outputs, 7 criteria scored 1–5, one non-blinded expert rater. **Overall NBP 197 / Seedream 179; portraits 68 / 59**; label 62 / 60; landscapes 67 / 60. Seedream's label looked "like a sticker stuck on the bag", and the image "cleaner but more like a mockup". Higgsfield cost: NBP 2 credits per image, Seedream 2.5 — [AI Pro Photography](https://www.aiprophotography.com/ai-photography-lab/nano-banana-pro-vs-seedream/)
- Other comparisons mostly favour NBP for skin. One found NBP's pore detail "a real step ahead" of GPT Image 2's "somewhat plastic" portraits, another that GPT Image 2 reads slightly "AI smooth" on close faces. **Contradicted** by an AI character-art comparison that picks GPT Image 2 for realistic portraits (more natural skin and expressions) and calls NBP portraits "too perfect". One production chose another model for photoreal casting because **Nano Banana gave more generic faces** **(snippet)** — [ChatCut](https://chatcut.io/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro), [Oakgen 20-prompt test](https://oakgen.ai/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro), [aigf.love (contradicting)](https://aigf.love/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-ai-characters/), [Apiyi](https://help.apiyi.com/en/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-which-ai-image-model-wins-en.html)
- Artificial Analysis Text-to-Image Arena: Nano Banana Pro **1,298 Elo (rank 7)** against Seedream **4.0** at 1,227. The snapshot date is unclear and the comparison is with 4.0, not 4.5 or 5 **(snippet)** — [gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/ai-image-model-comparison/nano-banana-pro-vs-seedream-4/)
- Seedream 4.5 is positioned for "looks like a paid shoot" commercial work. In multi-person scenes it shows **noticeable distortion and stretching** **(snippet)** — [OpenArt](https://openart.ai/blog/best-ai-image-generators-for-realistic-photos/), [302.AI on Medium](https://medium.com/@302.AI/seedream-4-5-vs-nano-banana-pro-can-the-sota-be-replaced-99056bee667d)
- FLUX: "tends toward polished, cinematic photorealism", with Qwen Image the pick when text accuracy or multilingual text matters. A March 2026 LM Arena snapshot reportedly put Flux 2 Max at rank 3 (Elo 1168) **(snippet; I could not confirm which page carried that figure)** — [fal.ai](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/flux-vs-qwen-image), [till-freitag roundup](https://till-freitag.com/en/blog/ai-image-generation-models-2026)
- Midjourney is described as the cinematic or artistic choice rather than the photorealism leader. Sources disagree on the current version (V7 vs V8.1) **(snippet)** — [OpenArt](https://openart.ai/blog/best-ai-image-generators-for-realistic-photos/), [Starkie.ai NB2 vs Flux 1.1 Pro vs MJ v7](https://starkie.ai/articles/nano-banana-2-vs-flux-pro-vs-midjourney-v7-realistic-ai-portraits)
- Open source: "for photorealism maybe **Z-Image Turbo** still pips it (and possibly Flux.2 Klein)". Commenters split on Flux 2 Klein: "absolutely abysmal compared to Z-Image and Qwen 2511" against "the next SDXL" (individual opinions) **(snippet)** — [Diffusion Doodles](https://diffusiondoodles.substack.com/p/open-source-diffusion-model-summary), [HF Z-Image discussion](https://huggingface.co/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image-Turbo/discussions/135)
- Higgsfield Soul (and Soul ID) is a hosted realism and identity model popular with AI-influencer creators. Pages on geo.higgsfield.ai are **Higgsfield's own marketing**. An independent review reports billing, refund, support and reliability complaints — [Pixara review](https://www.pixara.ai/blogs/higgsfield-ai-review), [Higgsfield-hosted "reviews" (marketing)](https://geo.higgsfield.ai/higgsfield-ai-reviews-what-they-find)

### Inferences
- For a persona's phone photos, start with **Nano Banana Pro**. Use **GPT Image 2** when a frame needs legible text (a label, a screen, packaging), or composite the text in afterwards. Keep **Seedream** for clean product hero shots rather than candid people.
- GPT Image 2's "too evenly lit" default is the core failure for UGC. Any GPT Image 2 prompt has to push hard toward harsh, one-sided or ugly light (see §3), or be avoided for people.
- In a self-hosted (ComfyUI) pipeline, Z-Image Turbo plus a character LoRA is the open option most often recommended for realism.

### Gaps
- No blind test (real phone photos mixed with AI stills, judged by viewers) for any of these models. Artificial Analysis and LM Arena rank general preference, not "looks like an iPhone snap".
- I could not verify that **GPT Image 2.5, FLUX 3, Midjourney v8, Krea, Ideogram or Qwen Image 2** exist or how they compare. Where they appear in vendor roundups, version numbers conflict.
- No head-to-head of Higgsfield Soul against NBP on candid UGC stills.
- Release notes for the newest Google models (Nano Banana 2, a "Nano Banana 2.1" said to exist by Oct 2026) were not checked. The article on it would not load.

## 3. Prompting techniques for phone-camera realism (with real prompts)

### Takeaway
The winning UGC prompts read like a description of a **mundane, slightly bad phone snap**, not a photo shoot. Name the camera *behaviour* (front-camera wide-angle or fisheye distortion, grain, hard or overhead or on-camera flash light, slight motion blur), make the subject and props **imperfect** (stubble, a faded hoodie, mediocre food), fill the background with **specific clutter**, and say outright that it should look like an Instagram Story snap and not an ad. Ask for skin texture with no smoothing, and ban the beauty filter. Lean, natural-language detail beats piles of camera jargon or fake "tokens".

### Cited Findings
**Prompts that won or were published**
- **The UGC selfie that won the NBP vs GPT Image 2 test** (9:16, quoted verbatim in parts): "Vertical phone selfie, front-camera aesthetic, slight fisheye distortion, mild grain, hard overhead kitchen LED lighting." / "A 28-year-old man with messy dark hair, a two-day stubble, and a faded black hoodie holds up a bowl of" / "mid-looking homemade ramen." / "The ramen has an overcooked egg, too many scallions, and a clearly frozen piece of packaged chashu." / "He is pulling a small self-aware grin at the camera." / "Background: a cluttered apartment kitchen — a pothos plant, a half-empty bottle of soy sauce," / "a rice cooker with the lid open." / "Image should look like an Instagram Story snap, not a food advertisement. **Intentional imperfection is the point.**" The prompt never says "iPhone" — [AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-2026/)
- **Skin cues from the winning hyperreal portrait**: "Visible skin pores, fine lines around the eyes, subsurface scattering in the lips, individual hair strands catching the light" + "No heavy retouching — this should read as a real person, not a beauty ad." (That prompt also used an 85mm f/1.4, Platon-style editorial look, which suits portraits, not UGC) — [AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-2026/)
- **Retiplex style line pasted into every generation**: "Vertical 9:16 video, 1080x1920. Casual UGC style, filmed on a phone, natural lighting, slightly imperfect." Creator portrait (Nano Banana): "29-year-old female, light olive skin, warm golden undertone, athletic muscular build", "medium textured dark brown hair," "warm brown eyes, natural smile lines," "in a home setting", aiming for a "natural phone-selfie feel rather than a studio headshot". Product still: "soft morning light from a window. Shot on a phone." Kling product-in-hand B-roll: "Clean, no filter, handheld phone video feel." Talking-clip (Veo/Grok) prompt: "The woman in this photo talks directly to the camera, natural head movement and blinking" / "like she is filming herself on her phone." / "Keep her face exactly as in the photo. Vertical 9:16." — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)
- **Oakgen's base persona prompt and negatives** **[vendor]**: "Woman in her early 30s, warm expression, natural skin texture, shoulder-length dark brown hair, simple cream sweater." with "no beauty-filter plastic skin, no exaggerated makeup, no text." — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)
- **Community Nano Banana Pro realism cues**: "harsh, direct on-camera flash lighting that creates hard shadows", "authentic film grain, slight motion blur, and chromatic aberration", "a candid, amateur snapshot" (a behind-the-scenes moment, not a studio shot); "realistic skin texture, no smoothing, no glossy finish"; JSON-style fields `"camera": "iPhone 15 Pro front camera"`, `"lens": "24mm wide"` with a "raw selfie" style. One guide says realism comes from believable light, a specific place, natural textures and camera-like framing, and **advises using camera terminology sparingly**. Google's own tip: "the key here is detail", covering lighting, angle and lens type **(snippet; community prompt galleries, not tested side by side)** — [awesome-nanobanana-pro (GitHub)](https://github.com/ZeroLu/awesome-nanobanana-pro), [Buldix casual smartphone prompt](https://www.buldix.com/prompts/casual-smartphone-photo-prompt-for-nano-banana-pro-12745), [Techpresso](https://academy.techpresso.co/prompts/nano-banana-prompts)
- **Older (Aug 2025) iPhone-style influencer prompts**: "photo captured with iPhone 15 Pro Max front camera, HDR mode, high exposure balance, soft bokeh background", "lightly edited with VSCO A6 filter, warm tones, natural lens distortion"; mirror selfie: "Shot on iPhone14.Pro.front.cam. Tokens: IMG_5101.CR2, iPhone_mirror.fx". Note the fake tokens ("real.camera.fx", "glamfilter.skinmap.2024"). They are prompt folklore with no evidence behind them, and "soft glowing skin" and "glam filter" push *toward* the beauty look you want to avoid — [MetricsMule](https://metricsmule.com/ai/ai-influencer-prompts/)
- **Amateur-look trigger words from an older Flux.1 LoRA**: "amateurish photo, low lighting, in motion, overexposed, underexposed, GoPro lens" **(snippet)** — [Flux Super Realism LoRA (HF)](https://huggingface.co/strangerzonehf/Flux-Super-Realism-LoRA/tree/90af669e02ecfff0faa12464a75f696d653db9d7)
- **Composition borrowed from real footage**: give the model a screenshot of a real creator's first frame and ask it to "recreate the scene's lighting, setting, and composition with the avatar in place of the original person" (paraphrased by the fetch tool, not quoted) — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- **What separates winners from losers**: GPT Image 2's UGC selfie failed because it was "too clean and evenly lit" and the food "too stylized", so flat even light and styled props are the main giveaways — [AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-2026/). Oakgen lists "overly polished, beauty-ad styling that removes UGC realism" as a cause of failure — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)

### Inferences
- A working template drawn from the winning prompts: **[device behaviour]** "vertical phone selfie, front-camera wide-angle with slight fisheye distortion, mild sensor grain, slightly blown highlights" + **[ugly or real light]** "hard overhead LED / harsh on-camera flash / window light from one side" + **[imperfect person]** age, stubble or blemishes, fine lines, flyaway hair, worn clothing + **[imperfect action and expression]** a half-smile or mid-word, the arm holding the phone implied + **[specific clutter]** 3–4 named everyday objects + **[intent line]** "should look like an Instagram Story snap, not an advertisement; intentional imperfection is the point" + **[negatives]** no beauty filter, no smoothing, no studio lighting, no text.
- Writing what an image *is not* ("not a food advertisement", "not a beauty ad") is a recurring device in the prompts that won.
- Lens-and-aperture jargon (85mm f/1.4, bokeh) belongs to editorial portraits and pulls *away* from the deep-focus, wide-angle look of a phone. For phone stills, describe the phone's behaviour instead.

### Gaps
- No controlled A/B evidence for single phrases ("iPhone 15 Pro" against "front-camera aesthetic", whether "HDR" helps or hurts, the effect of grain or noise words) on any model.
- No before/after pairs published with the prompts (the test pages show images I could not inspect, beyond the text verdicts).
- No published film-emulation or post-processing recipes for stills (adding real sensor noise, JPEG compression, phone-style sharpening) in the sources I reached.

## 4. Keeping one character consistent across shots

### Takeaway
For ad volumes (tens of shots), the common practice is **one locked hero still plus a small approved reference set (3–8 images), reused as an image reference in every generation, with one variable changed at a time**. Heavy users (AI influencers) add a **character LoRA** (15–50 images, often generated from that one base portrait with Nano Banana edits) or a hosted identity model (**Higgsfield Soul ID**). Whether a LoRA is still worth it is disputed: reference editing is enough for static ad shots, while a LoRA gives the closest match at volume.

### Cited Findings
- Skaleit: reuse **the exact same avatar image** as the input for every clip — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- Retiplex: generate the portrait once, save it, reuse it as the reference for every talking clip, and say "Keep her face exactly as in the photo" in the video prompt — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)
- Oakgen **[vendor]** (Jul 2026): write a **character bible** (identity; visual identity incl. "things to avoid"; wardrobe with approved alternates and banned colours; voice and delivery; behaviour/gestures; ad constraints incl. disclosure line; reference assets: **hero face, profile, full-body, talking-head crop, product-holding shot**). Approve **3–8 anchor images**; their example generates **30 stills, approves 5**. Isolate the character on a plain setting before any scenes. "Do not change age, hair, wardrobe, setting, and lighting all at once" — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)
- Oakgen's causes of drift: text-only prompts or weak references; **small faces, aggressive motion, dramatic camera moves**; lighting changes and long clips; wardrobe left unlocked; a different voice setting per clip; **switching models without a locked visual anchor**; beauty-ad styling; product warping. QA scores face, hair, wardrobe, voice, gesture, product accuracy, disclosure, claim safety and platform fit from 1 to 5, and **rejects a shot if face match < 4** — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)
- **Reference roster** (Oct 2026): give each uploaded reference one labelled role (subject, product, style, setting) and refer to it only by that label. **Two people and two products give cleaner results than using the maximum allowed.** Make one change per turn, because a label "can quietly rewrite itself by the third edit" **(snippet; page would not load)** — [UD (Nano Banana 2.1 consistency)](https://www.ud.hk/en/blogs/insight/article/nano-banana-21-consistency-2026-10-12)
- **Higgsfield Soul ID**: guides say train on **20–80 photos** (a few minutes) or 20+, while a mid-2025 test cites **10 photos (up to 70)**. An April 2026 Medium review says it locks the face reliably across shots. Pixara notes output still varies with lighting, expression and camera angle, and lists billing and support complaints. No quantified tests exist **(snippet)** — [302.AI test (Medium)](https://medium.com/@302.AI/higgsfield-soul-id-test-how-realistic-is-the-character-consistency-1bc8b3250bca), [CropUp guide](https://cropupai.com/blog/higgsfield-ai-prompting-guide/), [Pixara](https://www.pixara.ai/blogs/higgsfield-ai-review), [make-influencer.ai](https://make-influencer.ai/tools/higgsfield-ai)
- **LoRA on a generated persona**: datasets of **15–30** or **20–50** images ("train once, reuse forever"). Build the dataset from **one base portrait** (frontal or 3/4 upper body, neutral expression) edited into angles and expressions with Nano Banana, which one source calls "far better than Flux.1 Kontext" for this. Z-Image training settings in circulation: `network_dim: 84`, 15 epochs ("verified" per one guide), or rank 32 used at **0.75 weight** (another). A Z-Image tutorial says **Nano Banana Pro is worse at keeping identity than the non-Pro Nano Banana** for building datasets, which conflicts with guides that recommend Pro for realistic humans **(snippet)** — [Civitai Z-Image character LoRA guide](https://civitai.com/articles/23842/z-image-turbo-character-lora-training-guide-easy-how-to-guide), [zimage.run influencer workflow](https://zimage.run/blog/zi-074-ai-influencer-character-consistency-en-20260602), [lilys.ai AI-Toolkit notes](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/training-lora-20260209/master-z-image-lora-ai-guide), [AI Influencer Studio](https://www.aiinfluencerstudio.app/blog/character-lora-training-tutorial-for-ai-influencers-no-comfyui-required)
- **LoRA or references?** Segmind: "LoRA training for consistent character is dead", since Nano Banana matches or beats a LoRA for most static use. Rangy: reference images give "strong" consistency, LoRA "near-perfect" **(snippet)** — [Segmind](https://blog.segmind.com/lora-training-for-consistent-character-is-dead-2/), [Rangy](https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-character-consistency/)
- Face swap shows up only in passing (a third reference photo for the swap). I found no rigorous method — [Rangy](https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-character-consistency/) **(snippet)**

### Inferences
- A practical ladder: (1) one hero still plus 3–8 anchors as image references in NBP, one change per edit; (2) if the face drifts over 20+ shots, generate a 20–30 image dataset from the anchors and train a Z-Image or Flux LoRA; (3) keep faces large in frame and motion gentle at the video stage, where most drift happens.
- The "change one variable at a time" rule and the "two people and two products" cap both point the same way: references overloaded with many changes are the main cause of identity loss in reference-based models.

### Gaps
- No measured comparison (face-embedding similarity, blind identity rating) of references against LoRA against Soul ID against face swap.

## 5. Product in hand without warped labels

### Takeaway
Give the model a **clean, fully visible product photo** as a labelled reference, plus a written "visual profile" of the product. Tell it to keep the label, shape and colours identical, and **change one thing per edit**. Expect several rounds of corrective prompts. Pose the hand so it does not cover the label. For guaranteed accuracy, composite the real label or packshot back in. Keep camera motion small at the video stage, because motion warps products.

### Cited Findings
- A clean, fully visible product shot (label, logo and shape all visible) gives the model the most to anchor on. Cropped, blurry or filtered references give the least **(snippet)** — [Medium: Nano Banana for product photography](https://medium.com/ai-product-photography/how-to-use-nano-banana-for-product-photography-2025-guide-a0b91c6c928f)
- Corrective micro-prompts ("keep the hands as in the reference", "preserve product proportions", "make the label crisp and legible"). **Expect 3–10 rounds** per usable image (2025 guide) **(snippet)** — [Medium guide](https://medium.com/ai-product-photography/how-to-use-nano-banana-for-product-photography-2025-guide-a0b91c6c928f)
- Preservation wording in product templates: keep the label, shape and colours identical to the reference, with all text legible and exactly matching it **(snippet)** — [SurePrompts](https://sureprompts.com/blog/nano-banana-product-photography-prompts)
- Reference roster plus one change per turn to stop labels drifting by the third edit **(snippet)** — [UD](https://www.ud.hk/en/blogs/insight/article/nano-banana-21-consistency-2026-10-12)
- Retiplex: an LLM-written visual profile (container, colours, finish, material, **label text**) goes into every product prompt, with the real product photo. Check each generated product against the real one. The "keep" instruction is reserved for the face — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)
- In a scored test, both Nano Banana Pro and Seedream 5.0 Pro spelled a three-line coffee label correctly in 4 of 4 attempts. Seedream's label looked like a sticker, and NBP once split the brand name across lines and once cropped the bag — [AI Pro Photography](https://www.aiprophotography.com/ai-photography-lab/nano-banana-pro-vs-seedream/)
- GPT Image 2 is the stronger text renderer (most typography rounds), hence NBP for the base and GPT for text — [AI Video Bootcamp](https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-2026/)
- In Photoshop Generative Fill, Nano Banana output can shift out of alignment or distort, where Flux kept the original position. Users fix it with manual or Auto-Align Layers **(snippet)** — [Adobe community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/bug-photoshop-beta/generative-fill-with-gemini-2.5-(nano-banana)-is-giving-stretched-results/idi-p/15534785), [PhotoshopEssentials](https://www.photoshopessentials.com/photo-editing/how-to-fix-nano-banana-in-photoshop-2026/)
- At the video stage, products warp under "too much motion or no product reference" — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)

### Inferences
- Robust order: persona still → edit pass with the product reference ("she holds [product from image 2] at chest height, label facing camera, fingers below the label") → check the label → if it is wrong, patch the label region by inpainting or composite the real packshot (aligned, then relit and grained to match) → animate with low-motion prompts (Retiplex's "open palm, lift toward camera" beat is a good example).
- Fingers covering the label are the usual hand-held failure the guides do not address. Posing the hand away from the label avoids it.

### Gaps
- No source tests hand-held products specifically, or gives a measured warp rate across image-to-video models.
- No published compositing or relighting recipe (e.g. IC-Light) for putting real labels back into AI stills in a UGC context.

## 6. How agencies structure a UGC ad shoot list, and format conventions

### Takeaway
The standard spine is **talking-head hook (A-roll) → problem line → product reveal → demo or proof B-roll → benefit → talking-head CTA**, shot vertical at **9:16, 1080×1920, about 30 fps, H.264**, 10–20 s hook-led or 30–45 s VO-led. A typical character shot pack has about 7 shots that mix speaking frames and silent cutaways.

### Cited Findings
- Oakgen's **7-shot pack** **[vendor]**: front-facing talking-head intro; product held near face; over-the-shoulder product use; product close-up with hand interaction; post-use reaction; lifestyle cutaway without speech; final CTA talking-head — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-character-consistency-workflow)
- Retiplex: an 80-word, 30 s script split into **4 scenes** (shot type + VO line + duration, totalling 30 s), hook → agitate → reveal → proof → CTA; export 1080×1920 H.264 — [Retiplex](https://www.retiplex.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads)
- Skaleit: two formats, a **10–20 s hook-led** spot or a **30–45 s voiceover narrative**, plus a growing B-roll bank of every generated clip — [Skaleit](https://skaleit.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-cheap-realistic-meta-ads-method/)
- Sequence in the guides: talking-head hook → product shot for the reveal → faceless product B-roll for results → product shot for the CTA. A-roll outline: hook as a quick confession or bold claim, one relatable problem line, a demo or before/after proof, one feature with its benefit, a direct CTA. B-roll examples: **unboxing hands, product in a real setting, macro texture cut into a smiling reaction, a screenshot of a star rating with a short review line**. Specs: **1080×1920 at 30 fps, H.264 high bitrate**, **show the product by second 2**, burn in subtitles, **keep natural speech disfluencies**, avoid perfect studio conditions and unrealistic results **(snippet; spread across these guides, page-level attribution not verified)** — [Medium: Tech Thursdays UGC guide](https://medium.com/@gautsoni/tech-thursdays-a-practical-guide-to-making-scroll-stopping-ugc-ads-with-ai-adobe-reels-1ca46e0a164f), [Oakgen script-to-final-cut](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-video-ads-script-to-final-cut), [Vidau](https://www.vidau.ai/make-ai-generated-ugc-ads-look-authentic/), [Makeroll](https://makeroll.com/makeroll-ads/)
- Hook variants: tools advertise 50+ opening hooks per script, and hook text is judged by whether it reads in the first 1.5 s **(snippet, vendor)** — [Magicroll](https://magicroll.ai/product/ugc-ads), [Makeroll](https://makeroll.com/makeroll-ads/)
- Luma publishes 12 UGC-style video prompts for DTC ads **[vendor]** — [Luma](https://lumalabs.ai/news/ugc-style-ai-video-prompts)
- Seedance 2.0 makes 9:16 at 720p or 1080p in 5/10/15 s clips **(snippet)** — [ugcvids.ai](https://ugcvids.ai/compare/veo-3-1-vs-seedance-2-0)

### Inferences
- Plan stills per shot from the shot list: each A-roll line needs a talking-head still (face large, phone-at-arm's-length framing). Each B-roll beat needs a product-accurate still, which can be faceless, so hands and product only, and which tolerates text-to-video.
- Generate stills at 9:16 (or larger, then crop) so the start frame matches the 1080×1920 delivery and the video model does not have to re-frame.

### Gaps
- No agency case study with an actual shoot list, shot counts per ad, or the ratio of hooks tested. The structures above come from vendor and educational guides.
- No data on whether AI UGC with visible imperfections (disfluencies, messy rooms) outperforms polished AI UGC. That is asserted, not measured.
